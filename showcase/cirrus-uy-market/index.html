Uruguay is not a blank map.
It’s an empty hangar in someone else’s territory.

If Cirrus paid for a penetration study here, the first slide is not “open an office in Montevideo.” The country is already assigned to Cielo S.A. in San Fernando. The installed base looks near-zero. The money, if any, is seasonal Punta and a Southern Cone owner-pilot triangle — not a factory.

01 · Territory

Cielo already has Uruguay

Cirrus Argentina / Cielo S.A. publicly calls itself the authorized sales and service house for Argentina, Chile, Paraguay and Uruguay, based at San Fernando (SADF). Instagram: “representante oficial.”

Synerjet handles Cirrus in northern South America and Central America, and Pilatus across LATAM. Brazil is a different animal — ANAC certified the G3 Vision Jet on 6 Aug 2026.

So a UY “market entry” that ignores Cielo is stepping on the partner Cirrus already appointed.

02 · Installed base

Public CX Cirrus is a ghost

We can measure the US file. We cannot honestly count Uruguay without DINACIA. What is public: at least one CX-PPP SR22 on the Uruguayan register in the 2010s, later mentioned as sold into Argentina (2021–22 chatter). No active CX Vision Jet showed up on the open corporate-jet lists we checked.

Working assumption until a registry pull: 0–few Cirrus on CX. That is a penetration problem, or a data hole. Pay for the hole first.

03 · Demand

Punta is the market. Montevideo is the office.

Uruguay is 3.4 million people. The aviation money is not a national GA boom. It is Punta del Este (SULS) in season, Argentine and Brazilian wealth that already knows Cirrus from San Fernando and São Paulo, plus a thin owner-pilot class around Carrasco.

Infrastructure is moving: Aviasur FBO at Carrasco grew 600 → 1,500 m² (Aug 2025). Rocha airport is being framed as the next riviera strip (~US$40m land/plan talk). Charter today is Cessna / King Air language, not Cirrus language.

04 · Friction

No local service is the tax

A new SR22 owner in Atlántida or Punta still ferries to San Fernando for real Cirrus work. That is ~110 nm, trivial in the airplane, fatal in the sales pitch if you pretend otherwise.

Other open questions we would not fake on a paid slide: DINACIA type acceptance for current SR22T / SF50 G3, import + IVA, insurance, and how many local IFR owner-pilots can actually use the airplane.

If this were a 90-day paid scope

  1. Don’t open a UY company. Sit with Cielo. Ask for the Uruguay pipeline, lost deals, and who they already call in Punta.
  2. Buy the registry. DINACIA matrículas: Cirrus vs Cessna / Piper / Diamond / Tecnam. That’s the competitor slide.
  3. One demo weekend at SULS in season (Dec–Feb), not a year-round hangar. Owner-pilot SR22T, CAPS, Carrasco–Punta–San Fernando triangle.
  4. Productize the ferry. “Annual at SADF, we pick it up.” Makes the service desert honest instead of hidden.
  5. Pre-owned first. A used SR22 is how a thin market starts. New Vision Jet is a Brazil / Argentina story until DINACIA and service are clear.
