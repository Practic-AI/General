# Facts — FIFA / Balogun Disciplinary Outcome

Record only. No legal characterization. Updated for reported procedural detail.

## Background

- FIFA World Cup: U.S. striker Folarin Balogun receives a red card.
- Standard procedure triggers an automatic one-match suspension.

## Publicly reported sequence

1. Red card issued; automatic ban applies under FIFA disciplinary rules.
2. U.S. President Donald Trump publicly states he contacted FIFA President Gianni Infantino regarding the suspension.
3. Trump publicly requests review of the sanction.
4. FIFA disciplinary committee acts on Balogun's case.
5. **FIFA's stated outcome (per reporting):** committee does **not** characterize the ban as "lifted." It **suspends implementation** of the one-match ban under **Article 27** of the FIFA Disciplinary Code, fines U.S. Soccer **$40,000**, and attaches a reported condition that suspension holds only if Balogun avoids a similar foul for one year.
6. Balogun becomes eligible to play the next match.
7. Infantino states the disciplinary committee is independent and that he did not interfere.
8. Political figures publicly characterize the intervention as having produced the favorable outcome.
9. **UEFA** condemns the decision — reports describe language crossing a "red line" on integrity and credibility.
10. **Belgian federation** reportedly calls the decision unprecedented and unjustifiable.
11. Governance watchdogs (e.g., **FairSquare**) call for ethics investigation; FairSquare had filed a prior neutrality complaint against Infantino (December 2025, per reporting).

## FIFA's cited precedents (same tournament cycle, per reporting)

FIFA has pointed to prior **Article 27** deferrals this cycle as evidence of a rule-based pattern:

- **Cristiano Ronaldo** — ban deferred (reported: November 2025).
- **Nicolás Otamendi** — one-game ban deferred (reported: April 2026).
- **Moisés Caicedo** — one-game ban deferred (reported: April 2026).

**Reporting distinction:** No public record of a head-of-state call preceding those deferrals (as of available reporting).

## Pattern / governance context (public reporting)

- FairSquare neutrality complaint against Infantino (December 2025).
- Reporting on Infantino–Trump relationship: Club World Cup tickets (~$15,000, per reports); FIFA Peace Prize to Trump; 50 MEPs backing FairSquare's governance concerns (per reports).
- Post-Balogun: FairSquare director quoted (Reuters) on potential ethics-code violation if quid pro quo exists; broader critique of FIFA governance structure.

## Enforcement asymmetry — government interference (reviewed)

FIFA has **suspended national federations** for government interference while treating the Balogun sequence differently:

| Case | Date | Trigger (reported) | FIFA action |
|------|------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Kenya** | 25 Feb 2022 | Govt dissolved FKF, installed caretaker committee (Nov 2021) | Full suspension — no FIFA/CAF play, funding cut |
| **Zimbabwe** | 25 Feb 2022 | Govt SRC removed ZIFA leadership / took control | Same — announced alongside Kenya by Infantino |
| **Kenya lifted** | 28 Nov 2022 | Govt reinstated FKF executive | Suspension ended |
| **Zimbabwe lifted** | July 2023 | Governance conditions met (reporting) | Suspension ended before WCQ window |

**Contrast with Balogun:** head-of-state **call about one player's sanction** → Art. 27 deferral, player eligible (no federation suspension).

**Analytical use:** unequal-access / credibility comparator — not identical legal fact pattern. See `pattern-asymmetry.md`.
