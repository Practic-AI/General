# Pattern Evidence — Political Neutrality Asymmetry

**Status:** Reviewed against primary reporting (Feb 2022 suspensions + reinstatements).  
**Use:** Unequal-access / institutional-credibility argument — not a claim that Balogun is legally identical to Kenya/Zimbabwe.

---

## Why this comparator matters

FIFA's real defense on Balogun is **entry point**: Article 27 exists; other players got deferrals; the committee acted under rules.

The asymmetry evidence answers a different question:

> When **political power** touches football governance, does FIFA enforce neutrality **the same way** for a superpower head of state as for African federations?

It does not prove Trump "bribed" anyone. It shows **enforcement posture** — hard suspension when governments seize federations; accommodating sequence when a U.S. president calls about **one player's** red card.

## Kenya — government interference suspension (2022)

| Field | Record (sourced) |
|-------|------------------|
| **Trigger** | Nov 2021: Kenya Sports Cabinet Secretary dissolved Football Kenya Federation (FKF), installed caretaker committee |
| **FIFA action** | **25 Feb 2022** — suspended Kenya from all football activities for **government interference** |
| **Announced by** | Gianni Infantino (FIFA press conference) |
| **Consequences** | No FIFA/CAF matches; funding cut off; AFCON qualifying impact |
| **Lifted** | **28 Nov 2022** — after government reinstated FKF executive committee (Inside FIFA / reporting) |

**Prior pattern:** Kenya also suspended 2004 and 2006 over government/federation governance disputes (Wikipedia aggregate; separate triggers).

**Sources:** VOA (25 Feb 2022); The East African; Inside FIFA (lift); Wikipedia *FIFA ban on Kenya* (timeline cross-check).

## Zimbabwe — government interference suspension (2022)

| Field | Record (sourced) |
|-------|------------------|
| **Trigger** | Govt Sports and Recreation Commission (SRC) removed ZIFA leadership; SRC took control (Nov 2021–Jan 2022) |
| **Stated govt reasons** | Corruption, incompetence, sexual abuse allegations — FIFA said allegations should be investigated **without** government takeover |
| **FIFA action** | **25 Feb 2022** — suspended alongside Kenya, same announcement |
| **Consequences** | Warriors barred from FIFA/CAF competition; funding suspended |
| **Lifted** | **July 2023** — reported in time for World Cup qualifying window (Reuters) |

**Prior pattern:** Zimbabwe had **multiple** prior FIFA suspensions for government interference (long-running governance conflict — not one-off).

**Sources:** VOA (25 Feb 2022); Reuters (July 2023 lift); SRC/ZIFA public dispute reporting.

## Balogun — contrast (same institution, different direction)

| Field | Record (public) |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Political act** | U.S. President **admits calling** Infantino about **this player's** suspension; requests review |
| **FIFA outcome** | Committee acts; Art. 27 suspension of ban implementation; federation fined; player eligible |
| **Institutional response** | UEFA condemns; Belgian federation objects; ethics watchdogs mobilize |
| **FIFA framing** | Rule-based discretion + comparators (Ronaldo/Otamendi/Caicedo) |

**Reporting distinction:** No Kenya/Zimbabwe-style **suspension of a federation** — the political contact preceded a **favorable individual disciplinary outcome**.

## The asymmetry (plain English)

```
Small federation + government seizes control  →  FIFA: immediate ban, all football stopped
Superpower president + call on one red card   →  FIFA: review, Art. 27, player plays
```

FIFA's statutes and public posture treat **government interference in member associations** as a bright-line integrity violation. Infantino has also said he **regularly receives calls** from heads of state on many topics.

**Entry-point reframe (sharpest line):**

> "I take calls from leaders" ≠ "and that's how a **case-specific disciplinary review** gets triggered."

The gap between those two sentences is where the unequal-access argument should sit. It concedes Art. 27 exists; it does not concede **independent triggering**.

## Limits — do not overclaim

| Weak use | Strong use |
|----------|------------|
| "Balogun = same as Kenya ban" | Different fact patterns; same **credibility test** |
| "FIFA always corrupt" | **Selective enforcement posture** on political contact |
| Proof of quid pro quo | **Institutional inconsistency** supporting burden shift |

Kenya/Zimbabwe: government **replaced** federation leadership.  
Balogun: head of state **lobbied** on one sanction.  

Analogous principle (political neutrality), not identical proceeding.

---

## Cross-examination lines (Infantino)

1. On **25 Feb 2022**, you suspended Kenya and Zimbabwe for government interference. What is the FIFA rule that required that?
2. What is the difference — in FIFA's ethics framework — between a government **taking over** a federation and a head of state **calling you about one player's red card**?
3. You have said you regularly take calls from heads of state. How many of those calls resulted in **case-specific disciplinary review**?
4. Why was Kenya's federation suspended within months of government action, but the U.S. case received **Art. 27 relief** after a presidential call?
5. Name the neutral criterion that explains **both** outcomes as equally consistent with FIFA independence.
