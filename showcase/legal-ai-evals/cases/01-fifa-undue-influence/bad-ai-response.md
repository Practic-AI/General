# Bad AI Response — Tagged Errors

Representative deficiencies from a generic model exchange on this controversy. Summarized and tagged — not a verbatim transcript.

## What the model said (pattern)

> "The criticism is about **appearance** of political influence, not necessarily corruption."
>
> "You have **correlation**, not **causation** — the review might have happened anyway."
>
> "To prove corruption you need **statutory elements** — bribery, honest services fraud, FCPA."
>
> "Whether it's pathetic depends on your perspective."
>
> "A president commenting on sports is normal; the issue is only if **preferential treatment** followed."

## Error tags

### 1. Wrong lead jurisdiction
Leads with U.S. federal bribery statutes. The primary frameworks are **FIFA governance rules** and **influence/ethics doctrine** — not 18 U.S.C. § 201.

### 2. False equivalence — appearance vs. substance
Treats "looks bad" and "is improper" as separate when **admissions already establish intervention in a specific case**. The call is not rumor; it is record.

### 3. Causation dodge
"Review might have happened anyway" ignores:
- Bespoke access (head of state → FIFA president → this case).
- Temporal sequence (sanction → call → review → lift).
- Selectivity (ordinary fans do not obtain this pathway).

### 4. Burden misallocation
Places full proof burden on critics pre-discovery. When a powerful actor intervenes in a supposedly independent process and the outcome favors their interest, **the institution must explain independence** — not the public.

### 5. Missing anchor question
Never asks: **Why was this intervention entertained at all?**

### 6. Hedging as substitute for analysis
"Depends on your perspective" and "everybody reacts strongly" replace structured element mapping.

## What a tutor should flag

A training-quality response must **name theories**, **map elements to admitted facts**, **identify burden shift**, and **distinguish criminal conviction from institutional impropriety** — without retreating to correlation lectures when admissions exist.
