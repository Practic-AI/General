# Say this on a walk. No slides.

One sentence first:

> This is every Cirrus that still has a US tail number today. Not what the factory sold. Not where they fly tonight. Just the paperwork.

Then six beats. If they only remember three, keep 1, 3, and 5.

## 1. What the file is
The FAA publishes the whole US aircraft register. We kept Cirrus, threw away owner names and addresses. About **9,400** airframes.

If someone says “so that’s the Cirrus fleet,” you say: **US N-numbers only.** Europe is invisible.

## 2. The mix
Mostly pistons.

- SR22 — the classic, biggest pile
- SR22T — turbo, took over newer years
- SR20 — smaller / trainer-ish
- SF50 Vision Jet — the little jet, about 1 in 12

You do not need the exact percents. “Mostly 22s, jet is the small gold slice.”

## 3. The 2009 hole
Look at year built, not year sold. 2006 is a fat year. **2009 is a crater.** That’s the financial crisis still sitting in the metal. Then the turbo shows up, then the jet. 2026 looks short because the year is not over.

This is *survivors on the US register*, not GAMA deliveries. Cirrus says they have delivered about 11,000 SR Series worldwide. This file has about 8,600 SR still wearing a US tail. The missing ones were exported, wrecked, or deregistered.

## 4. Minnesota is not 553 happy owners
About **360** planes are status `M` — still on the factory dealer certificate. Almost all of those sit in **Minnesota**. That’s inventory / new airplanes, not “Minnesotans love Cirrus 2x more than Florida.”

Florida, California, Texas are the real flying markets.

## 5. Paper homes
**Delaware** is huge. **Wyoming** punches above its population. People title airplanes in LLC-friendly states. The plane can live in a hangar in Arizona.

**63%** of the file is an LLC, not “Fernando Pazos” on the registration. That’s insurance and liability, not a personality test.

## 6. The messy 8%
About **8%** of rows have no manufacture year. If you ever age the fleet or price residuals off this file and you ignore that, you are lying to yourself. That is the data-quality answer. Not Great Expectations. Just: *count the blanks.*

## What you can guess from this (honest)

| You can say | You cannot say |
|---|---|
| How the US paper fleet is mixed | How many hours each plane flew |
| Which years still survive here | What Cirrus booked in revenue |
| Factory stock is sitting in MN | Where the airplane is parked tonight |
| LLCs and DE/WY are a titling trick | Who the owner is (we deleted that) |
| Jet share of *new* vintages | Whether the jet is “winning” worldwide |
| The register is dirty (missing years) | Residual value of an SR22 |

## What we could bolt on later (still public)

- **GAMA / Cirrus press deliveries** vs this register → “sold vs still on a US tail”
- **ADS-B** (OpenSky) → who actually flies, not who holds the N-number
- **Airports / service centers** → does the fleet sit near Cirrus service?
- **State pilots / income / population** → why Florida isn’t a mystery
- **NTSB** → only if we are careful; accident tourism is a bad look for this application

## How to demo the hangar

Open `play/Open.bat` (or `play/index.html`).

1. Click through the story cards like you’re showing a friend a hangar.
2. Drag the mouse over the dotted US map — the lights in that state flicker.
3. Hit **Surprise me** — a random tail pops up.
4. Turn models on/off. Drag the year bar. Click a state to lock the filter.
5. Search an N-number if they ask “can I look one up?”

If it gets stiff, say: *we built this because the public file is sitting there and it’s fun, not because Cirrus sent us a spreadsheet.*
